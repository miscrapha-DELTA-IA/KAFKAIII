# Supplementary developmental analysis: KAFKA III v1.9G (12 November 2026)

**Status:** internal, exploratory and synthetic. This addendum was written after observing the reported results; it is not a pre-registered confirmatory analysis. It must not replace the original negative results or claim superiority over existing safe policy learning methods.

We investigated whether a temporally aware authority gate could reduce regret under possible latent-effect changes. The experiment used 22 synthetic environments, 8 policies, 800 paired episodes per environment, and six decisions per episode (140,800 policy trajectories). We preserved the first 18 generators from version 1.9E and added four stochastic Markov-switching families. The shared exposure set was {0, .25, .5, .75, 1} with no more than two intermediate-exposure periods. The observation channel remains an **abstract synthetic signal** and does not identify the causal effect of displayed clinical alerts; the q0=.02 nonzero signal rate at extreme exposure makes this limitation particularly salient.

The new KAFKA temporal gate employed Bayesian model averaging over switching hazards {0, .2, .4} and sensor accuracies {.55, .85}, followed by a model-consensus gate for extreme exposure. Controls included static nominal Bayesian dynamic programming, static accuracy mixture dynamic programming, switching Markov dynamic programming (hazard .2), model-average Markov dynamic programming, a myopic worst-model rule, the previous KAFKA gate and a conservative static proxy. The model-average planner is Bayesian-optimal **within the specified static model-mixture class with one-period signal maturation and numerical rounding**, not a robust POMDP minimax optimum. The Markov filters reconstruct delayed evidence at its original cohort timestamp.

The KAFKA temporal gate yielded smaller average simulated loss than the dynamic model-average Bayes comparator in 4 of the 22 families, and smaller loss than the correctly specified Markov-hazard .2 planner in 11 families. It did not have the smallest observed mean loss among all eight methods in any family. In the `benefit_to_harm` generator, KAFKA averaged 0.3393 loss versus 0.3545 for the Bayesian model average; the paired comparator-minus-KAFKA difference was 0.0151, with a descriptively adjusted 95% Monte Carlo Bonferroni interval [0.0010, 0.0292] across 154 prespecified family-comparator contrasts. Other environments showed material harm from overcautious gating, notably persistent harm, harm-to-benefit reversal, and the Markov switching families. These Monte Carlo intervals describe simulation noise only; they do not account for earlier research iteration, structural uncertainty, or clinical transportability.

The exercise does not establish KAFKA's incremental methodological superiority. In particular, a Bayesian optimizer cannot be uniformly beaten under the same correctly specified model, actions, information, and expected-loss criterion. We therefore recommend decoupling (i) *causal evidence identification*, (ii) *dynamic information-aware planning*, and (iii) *baseline-relative safety constraints*. A conditional safety frontier defined by `max_{delta in [L_t,U_t]} (e0_t - e_t)*delta <= eta_t` gives a simple bound on cumulative expected regret relative to reference exposure whenever all future effects lie in their defensible envelopes, with no interference and separate accounting of operational costs. This is a standard consequence of baseline-relative policy learning, not a novel theorem.

The full 22-family table, 154 paired contrasts, 19 new tests, protocol hash, and source code are included in the v1.9G/v1.9H technical archive. Any superiority evaluation must next use an identified alert-display estimand and an independent prospective benchmark with the same authorization and safety constraints for all methods.
